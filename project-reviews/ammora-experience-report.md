# Ammora: Testnet Participation and User Experience Report

## 1. Project Overview

Ammora is a Web3 project focused on programmable liquidity markets within the GIWA ecosystem.

This report documents my personal experience participating in Ammora's waitlist and testnet activities. It focuses on user experience, activity tracking, and opportunities to improve product communication.

## 2. My Participation Experience

I participated in Ammora's waitlist and mission activities to explore the product and understand its user experience.

My recorded progress at that time was:

- Waitlist position: 1,088
- Ammora Points (AP): 6,640

These figures represent my personal records at the time of participation. They are not current platform statistics.

## 3. Points and Waitlist Experience

The waitlist and AP system provided a way to track participation progress.

From a user perspective, clear progress tracking helps participants understand whether their activities have been recorded and which tasks remain incomplete.

Points should not be treated as guaranteed token allocations or financial rewards unless the project officially confirms the relevant terms.

## 4. Bridge Activity

I attempted the bridge-related activity as part of my participation.

In my experience, the bridge count did not appear to increase after approximately two or three attempts.

This is a personal observation. I could not independently establish the technical cause or confirm whether other users experienced the same behavior.

A clearer activity status and transaction history could help users understand whether their actions have been completed and credited.

## 5. Swap Experience

I also explored the swap functionality.

During my attempts, the swap process stopped instead of completing as expected.

I could not determine the underlying cause from my observations. Further investigation would require transaction details, error messages, and relevant testnet activity.

Clear feedback could help users understand whether a swap is pending, unsuccessful, or completed.

## 6. Key User Experience Observations

Based on my personal experience, three areas are worth examining:

1. **Progress tracking:** Users need clear information about whether bridge activity has been recorded.
2. **Transaction feedback:** A stopped swap should provide an understandable status or error message.
3. **User guidance:** Clear instructions can help beginners distinguish between pending transactions, failed transactions, and activities that have not been credited.

These are user experience observations, not confirmed technical findings.

## 7. Recommendations

The following suggestions could improve the experience for testnet participants:

- Show a clear status for bridge and swap attempts.
- Provide an activity history users can review.
- Explain how long progress verification may take.
- Display actionable error messages when an operation fails.
- Provide a simple troubleshooting guide for common issues.

These recommendations are based on the issues I encountered and are intended as constructive feedback.

## 8. Limitations

This report documents one participant's experience. It is not a security audit, technical diagnosis, or comprehensive product review.

The observations have not been independently reproduced, and I have not established the root cause of the reported behavior.

The waitlist position and AP figures are historical personal records and may no longer reflect the current account state.

## 9. What I Learned

Participating in a Web3 testnet involves more than completing tasks. It also provides an opportunity to observe how a product communicates transaction status, records user activity, and guides participants through unfamiliar workflows.

Documenting these experiences helped me practice product observation, user-focused writing, and constructive feedback.

These are skills I want to continue developing as a Web3 content creator and community contributor.
